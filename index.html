About

Building structures.
Understanding behaviour.

I am interested in understanding how structures respond, fail, and can be designed more intelligently.

My academic work sits at the intersection of structural mechanics, numerical simulation and computational methods.

I work primarily with finite element modelling, structural analysis and Python-based automation, with particular interest in steel structures, cold-formed members, imperfections and computational approaches to structural design.

Alongside research, I explore how machine learning and computational tools can complement conventional engineering methods.

02
Research

Computational approaches to structural behaviour.

My research interests focus on numerical modelling, structural instability, geometric imperfections and computational methods for engineering analysis.

R01

Structural Instability

Numerical investigation of buckling behaviour in steel and cold-formed structural members, including the influence of imperfections, perforations and stiffening.

R02

Finite Element Modelling

Development and analysis of nonlinear finite element models using ABAQUS for structural mechanics and connection behaviour.

R03

Computational Mechanics

Python-based computational workflows for model generation, simulation, post-processing and interpretation of structural response.

R04

Data-Driven Engineering

Exploring machine learning and data-driven modelling as complementary tools for prediction and engineering design.
